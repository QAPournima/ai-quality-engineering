How quality evolved — and how I evolved with it

Technology changes. Software changes. Engineering practices change. Quality has to evolve with them.

The definition of quality evolved with the technology, and I evolved with it.

I started my QA career in 2010. I did not leave one version of QA behind to move into another. I experienced the evolution.

2010 Automation Systems Quality engineering AI Today

2010 — Start with the fundamentals

My career started at Wipro, working on British Telecom projects. The focus was understanding requirements, designing test cases, executing functional and regression testing, identifying defects and validating that the software met expectations.

The technology of the time shaped the way we worked. It taught me something that has stayed with me:

Tools change. Good engineering judgement doesn’t.

Understanding requirements. Thinking about risk. Questioning assumptions. Exploring what could go wrong. Communicating clearly. Those fundamentals became the foundation for everything that followed.

The first shift — automation

As applications and release cycles became more complex, repeating everything manually became increasingly difficult. Automation became part of the engineering workflow — not simply replacing manual execution, but creating repeatability, faster feedback and confidence.

The question evolved from “Can I test this?” to:

How can I create reliable feedback every time this changes?

That shift took me deeper into test automation and engineering, through programmes at Tech Mahindra / TalkTalk and IVL / Verizon.

The system got bigger

Experience expanded across web, mobile, APIs, backend services and distributed systems. At Kapten / FreeNow the passenger product included Android, iOS, web, APIs, microservices and a database. Testing one screen was no longer enough. I needed to understand how an action travelled through the system.

  1. User action
  2. Application
  3. API
  4. Service
  5. Database
  6. Response
  7. User experience

Quality wasn’t just about checking the final screen. It was about understanding the system behind the screen.

Mobile changed the game again

Different devices, operating systems, screen sizes, permissions, network conditions, and real users doing unexpected things. I moved deeper into mobile automation with Appium and device farms, and later Maestro. The technology changed. The principle remained: build feedback that helps engineers understand whether the product can be trusted.

QA became a team sport

As Engineering Team Lead for QA Automation at Aircall, the question was no longer only “Did I test it?” It became “How does the team build quality?” Requirements, test strategy, automation, risk, collaboration, release confidence, engineering practices, continuous feedback.

Quality became less about a final checkpoint and more about how engineering teams work together — including CI/CD, canaries and observability on the path to production.

Aircall timeline →

Then AI arrived

AI isn’t simply another testing tool. It is changing how engineers can interact with software development. I started experimenting across requirement analysis, test design, test generation, automation, LLM testing, failure investigation, prompt engineering, AI agents and software development.

I did not have to abandon what I had learned. The fundamentals still mattered. AI gave me another way to apply them.

Today — quality + engineering + AI

Work sits at the intersection of AI × software engineering × quality engineering. The loop looks like this:

  1. Understand
  2. Ask AI
  3. Challenge
  4. Build
  5. Test
  6. Observe
  7. Investigate
  8. Improve

Claude, OpenCode, LLM Gateway, Playwright, Maestro, React, TypeScript, Python, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Docker and Datadog show up in that loop. The important part is not the list. It is adapting how I engineer quality as the technology changes.

I don’t look back at earlier stages of QA and think they were the wrong way. They were the right approach for the products, technology and engineering practices of the time. Then technology moved forward. So did we.

When technology changes, quality changes with it.

From 2010 to today that is one continuous evolution — not a series of disconnected QA careers. Employed tenure across those years is 13+ years. The story is adaptation, not a restart.
